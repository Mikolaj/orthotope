1.6 RTS-SOURCE VERDICTS, from a source-reading pass over the GHC checkout
at horde-ad/ghc (HEAD d415f38a75; the behaviors of interest reproduce
on 9.12.4). The load-bearing facts, each with its source anchor so none
is re-derived: (a) AT -A >= 16m THE NURSERY IS SILENTLY CHUNKED INTO 4 MB
CHUNKS, and the setting OVERRIDES an explicit -n (rts/RtsFlags.c:1997-2000,
normaliseRtsOpts, "If we have -A16m or larger, use -n4m", since 2016). So -A32m
runs as 8 chunks and -A1G as 256, handed out in index order, GC when all
are spent -- any model assuming one monolithic -A1G chain is wrong at the first
fork, and the planned -n discriminators were inert as designed (2.5 rewritten
below). (b) The nursery is born as a chain of SINGLE-block groups (contiguous
only by startup accident); resetNurseries keeps blocks and order; but every
out-of-line allocate() and every new pinned accumulator block MOVES a block
to the front of the chain (rts/sm/Storage.c:1156-1188, the ASCII-diagram
comment), and nothing ever re-sorts it. Trim/grow after GC frees from the head
and refills "from a random block" of the free list -- GHC's own Note [Sources
of Block Level Fragmentation] (Storage.c:1884-1925, tickets 19481/19248)
documents the "hodge-podge" decay and names the not-implemented fix
(periodically reallocate the nursery). (c) Small pinned (total object < 409
words, i.e. payload <= ~3256 B with the 2-word header) shares a per-capability
accumulator block from a dedicated pinned_object_empty pool refilled ~1 MB
at a time from the block allocator -- NOT from nursery blocks, though each new
pinned block advances the nursery pointer for GC accounting
(Storage.c:1239-1254). A 2304-byte vector fills an accumulator block ALONE (two
don't fit in 4 KB), so cnn-slice-c32's ~1M sprays churn ~1M singleton blocks:
retired to gen-0's large-object list, freed at the GC that collects their
generation, into the LIFO size-binned free lists. Pinned allocation fully counts
toward the GC trigger. (d) Free lists: per-node power-of-two buckets, LIFO push,
O(1) neighbor coalescing, allocation takes the head of the first bucket
that fits and splits from the HIGH-address end; NO address ordering,
NO rebuilding, NO defragmentation, ever -- documented as a conscious trade-off
("nothing you can do about it beyond hoping the pinned data is eventually
freed"). A >= 1 MB group (every own-group result at l >= ~131k) lives and dies
at megablock granularity on a separate ADDRESS-SORTED best-fit list -- which
is why the big-result shapes should scramble far less per byte. (e) Kernel-time
face: memory returns to the OS only at MAJOR GC (madvise(MADV_FREE) per freed
run), and regrowth RE-MMAPS the range (MAP_FIXED, fresh zero pages) plus
MADV_WILLNEED -- so a shrink-regrow cycle converts into minor faults
and page-table churn that user-CPU cannot see. -Fd's decay advances
on non-overflow majors (criterion does one per bench); -I0 removes the idle
majors that would otherwise drive it. Note [Scaling retained memory] (GC.c:2310)
and Note [-Fd and thrashing] (GC.c:2361, ticket 21483) are the documented policy
and its documented failure mode -- the -A32m/imagenet pathology of 2.9
is plausibly that Note's case. (f) Observability:
gcdetails_block_fragmentation_bytes in GHC.Stats is a direct free-pool gauge
((committed - allocated) bytes, rts/Stats.c:494-496); eventlog has
HEAP_SIZE/BLOCKS_SIZE/MEM_RETURN events (MEM_RETURN fires every major GC);
a -debug RTS's -Dg prints a per-GC memory inventory (nursery / empty pinned /
free pool) -- structure-inspection only, a debug RTS is not the timed binary
(horde-ad docs/ghc-issue-debug-changes-codegen.md). (g) Negative controls
from source: compacting GC (-c) skips BF_PINNED blocks and touches no free list;
performGC/rts_clearMemory restructure nothing (clearMemory only zeroes); no GC
ever memcpy's a pinned or large object -- live ones are relinked whole. -G1
makes every GC major (pinned freed every GC) and resizes the nursery every GC.
(h) Design-intent ledger inputs: free-list non-repair = documented trade-off;
pinned-never-moved = interface guarantee; the object-in-block retention hazard
of shared pinned blocks = UNREMARKED in the sources (accident-shaped); OS-return
policy = thoroughly documented with tickets; nursery-contiguity decay =
documented, fix named and not implemented.

1.7 MECHANISM CANDIDATE, registered before any further measurement, refutable
by 2.1/2.4/2.5: the poison is per-iteration SMALL-pinned churn -- each sub-3276
B result occupies a whole 4 KB accumulator block, so ~1M iterations push ~1M
singleton blocks through gen-0 into the LIFO buckets, scrambling the singleton
free lists and, via nursery trim/grow and accumulator-pool refills
(allocLargeChunkOnNode takes any group >= 1 block), degrading the ADDRESS LAYOUT
under the victim's nursery and pinned recycling without moving any counter GHC
reports -- LLC misses only, dTLB flat, mem-in-use flat, permanent, -H2G-immune
(pre-sizing neither sorts buckets nor de-permutes the nursery). The -A grading
would then be the victim-side walk: per-sample nursery reuse at 4 MB fits L3
whatever its order, at 32 MB sits at the L3 boundary, at 1 GB the walk is long
and prefetch-dependent. Predictions this stakes: the corrected scan's strength
tiers (1.2); dose dependence (2.4); a discontinuity or slope change across
the -A15m/-A16m chunking boundary (2.5); block_fragmentation_bytes
and BLOCKS_SIZE moving during the poison phase while mem-in-use barely does
(2.5).

4. DEFECT OR CONSCIOUS TRADE-OFF -- the ledger, design-intent side now filled
   from 1.6h, mechanism side awaiting 2.1-2.7. Per root cause:
(i) Unnamed position term -- mechanism registered at 1.7, not yet confirmed.
The design-intent reading splits it: the free lists' never-repaired scatter
is a DOCUMENTED trade-off (Note [Sources of Block Level Fragmentation]: "nothing
you can do about it"), but the specific ingredient 1.7 leans on -- a single
small live-or-recent pinned object cycling whole 4 KB blocks through the pool --
is UNREMARKED in the sources, accident-shaped. The nursery-reallocation fix
GHC's own Note names and never implemented is the natural upstream remedy
to propose. Filable only with 2.7's reproducer plus the confirmed mechanism.
Real-program -A1G exposure: YES, potentially large -- the one root cause
that taxes a later phase of an ordinary program permanently. (ii) Promotion
below the live span (the five gainers, 0.42 ms/MB) -- generational design
working as intended; trade-off; costs small nurseries, not -A1G. (iii) The 20.9
MB gen-1 growth budget cadence -- design. (iv) Kernel time when per-call excess
outruns the nursery, and its non-monotone -A32m face at l=28.9M -- the policy
is thoroughly documented (Note [Scaling retained memory]) and its failure mode
too (Note [-Fd and thrashing], ticket 21483): a documented trade-off whose edge
2.9 checks we are on. Real-program exposure at -A1G: none (a small-nursery
cost). (v) -A1G under a -M cap turning minors into majors -- configuration
interaction, by design, but a footgun worth documenting. (vi) Peak-inversion
at scale -- arithmetic of (ii); design. (vii) The ~1.2 s/GB first-touch
of a large area -- OS cost, design; a startup cost for -A1G programs
and a small-n benchmark trap. (viii) The silent -n4m override at -A >= 16m
(1.6a) -- a deliberate default, but one that silently overrides an EXPLICIT -n,
which is at least a documentation defect; it also means every -A32m/-A1G claim
in these pages is really a claim about a 4 MB-chunked nursery of that total
size. (ix) Work item 27601 -- background per Mikolaj; not pursued here.
